# Multiplayer scaling plan: stages 0 to 6, from 8 players a world to 100

An implementation plan of 2026-11-11, refreshed on 2026-11-12 against the code as the performance
review of pull requests #695 to #697 left it, for stages 0 to 6 of the
[multiplayer scaling report](multiplayer_scaling_report.md): measure, authority hygiene,
shared-world rules, replication, splitting the authority, crowds on phones, and persistence and
operations. It was built from a survey of the code, research on each stage's proven methods, and
the owner's twelve decisions. The research notes that its tables name in their *From* columns are
not kept in the repository. Its finding in a line: Pellagos reaches 100 players merge by merge.

Pellagos gets from 8 players a world on deterministic lockstep to 100 on server-authoritative replication without a flag day, because the authority (`Host`) never changes: a per-world `netcode` setting chooses which feed a world's players receive, and every lockstep client's exact copy of the world checks the new replicated view field by field in shadow mode before anyone depends on it. The work is **178 pull-request-sized slices in eleven tracks under one numbering**. It runs in this order: measurement and Stage 6's metrics first; authority hygiene; then three tracks side by side (shared-world rules proven on 8-player worlds, the client's two seams, and the public-world safety, operations and ledger work brought forward from Stage 6); then replication in dark, shadow and canary steps; crowds on phones; instance threads; and the storage cut-over last. Four gates control exposure: **(a)** the first replicated world opens at 8 players by key; **(b)** a public replicated world rises above 8 only when every safety slice, Nanny Mode, the trade ledger, the restore drill and the legal checklist are done; **(c)** browser-hosted games move to replication after four weeks of server worlds on it; **(d)** lockstep is deleted. The owner's decisions change the researchers' recommendations in four large ways: no lockstep world ever rises above 8; typed chat for everyone at opening makes moderation and the UK and US legal duties a hard precondition of gate (b); **Nanny Mode** holds a player who connects from the United Kingdom, Brazil, Indonesia or an unknown country to pre-canned phrases, sent and received, until an age check passes, and the chat panel says so, with an icon beside each such player and the way to remove it; and player against player becomes opt-in early in Stage 2, because it already exists today between players of peoples at war. Nothing here spends money without approval: the priced approval points are a lawyer's flat-fee review (about $350 to $2,500), a domain (about $10 to $11 a year) and five optional items, among them the age-check provider that alone can lift Nanny Mode (about $0.80 to $1.50 a check at published prices), each set beside its free alternative. Every acceptance number is a proposal until Stage 0's baselines exist on the owner's M4 Pro and iPhone, and every size or duration is an estimate. The performance review of 11 November 2026 (pull requests #695, #696 and #697) built two of the 39 slices of phases 1 and 2 (`1-1` and `1-6`), part-built seven (`F-1`, `6A-2`, `0-11`, `1-4`, `1-5`, `1-9`, `1-10`) and changed the shape of three (`F-2`, `1-3`, `1-12`); outside those phases it built `3-10`. Each such row says so at the start of its *What changes* cell, with the commit and what remains, and no slice was renumbered.

## The programme at a glance: eight phases, four gates, eleven approvals

The plan was built from ten research notes written on 11 November 2026 against `main` at `84f08e24` (`NET_VERSION` 223, `SNAPSHOT_VERSION` 52), the tenth on Nanny Mode after the owner's twelfth decision, and from the earlier report, [*Scaling Pellagos worlds to MMO*](multiplayer_scaling_report.md). It was refreshed on 12 November 2026 against `main` at `c601d300` (`NET_VERSION` 224, `SNAPSHOT_VERSION` 53), after the three-part performance review merged as pull requests #695, #696 and #697: every slice of phases 1 and 2 was checked against the new code, and every code link was re-read or re-anchored at that commit. It stands on its own; where a note and an owner's decision differ, the decision wins and the adjustment is stated. Code is cited as relative links, so `[netplay.js:156](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L156)` opens the line. Slice ids carry a prefix per stage: `F` for the foundations that run through every stage, `0` to `5` for Stages 0 to 5, and `6A` (observability), `6B` (public-world safety), `6C` (operations) and `6D` (persistence) for Stage 6, which the plan splits. Each table's *From* column names the note and the note's own id, because the notes reuse the same letters for different things (there are three different slices called S1 and two called M1). Ids are stable: a slice added after the first version takes the next free number in its track and sits in its table in work order, which is why `2-36` and `2-37` appear straight after `2-9`.

The order of work is not the order of the stage numbers. Stage 6's observability comes first because every gate is a measurement. Stage 6's safety and operations work was recommended to sit beside Stage 2 when the notes assumed a public lockstep world of 12 to 16; decision 3 removes that world, so this work is spread across phases 3 to 5 and has to finish only before gate (b). Stage 5 runs beside Stages 3 and 4 because it touches no netcode. Lockstep's deletion comes last and depends on nothing in Stage 4.

## Foundations: one authority, two feeds, and a switch for every step

### How multiplayer stays playable at every merge

The studios that changed netcode in a live game shipped the new mode beside the old one, chosen per match or per connection, tested it inside the live game and widened it by share with a switch to turn it off. Path of Exile added lockstep beside its predictive mode as a choice between two ([GGG, 2015](https://ru.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/1262596)); For Honor's separate test servers drew 1.6% of players while tests inside the live game with real progression drew 21 to 26%, and its use of branches instead of feature switches caused "merge hell" ([Ubisoft, 2021](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/219qSeV0Zh33JOI9cKY4ly/how-for-honor-made-testing-a-celebrated-player-experience)); Dead by Daylight moved to dedicated servers by a small share of players raised over time ([Behaviour, 2019](https://devtrackers.gg/deadbydaylight/p/5df66adf-status-update-dedicated-servers)). The method underneath is branch by abstraction, which requires that "the system builds and runs correctly at all times" ([Fowler, 2014](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/BranchByAbstraction.html)), with a parallel run in the manner of GitHub's Scientist, which runs old and new on the same input, returns the old result and records mismatches until they reach zero ([GitHub, 2016](https://github.blog/developer-skills/application-development/scientist/)). Pellagos has an advantage none of them had: **the same `Hosting` runs in a browser and in the server's world thread** ([together.js:525](../client/js/app/together.js#L525), [world-worker.js:106](../server/world-worker.js#L106)), the authority does not change, and a lockstep client's copy is exact, so any structural difference between the copy and the replicated mirror is a bug and not noise.

Eight mechanisms carry the programme. **A mode per world**: `netcode` joins `WORLD_SETTINGS` ([worlds.js:104-111](../server/storage/worlds.js#L104-L111)) and is changed only while nobody is in, as the character rule is ([worlds.js:98-101](../server/storage/worlds.js#L98-L101)); the socket's protocol is already named to the client in `/enter`'s answer ([api.js:292](../server/api.js#L292), [play.js:32](../server/play.js#L32)), so lockstep stays `pellagos.play.1` and views become `pellagos.play.2`. **Capability negotiation**: the client says what it speaks; a world it cannot speak refuses it in words, and those words ship a release early because "a refusal's words are read by games of every version" ([netplay.js:895-896](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L895-L896)). **The kill switch**: the server setting `replication` flips on the management page with no deploy; at `off` a replicated world refuses entry with its save intact, and a world of 8 or fewer reopens as lockstep after one set-aside, because the save is the same `Host` snapshot whichever way players are connected ([keeper.js:1-6](../server/keeper.js#L1-L6)). **Two client seams**: a link seam over the 12 members of `this.remote` that `game.js` uses, and a read seam (the "world view") over the 64 `host` members it reads in 221 places. **Dark launch, then shadow mode**: the server first builds views and only counts them; then a lockstep client that asks is also sent its view stream and compares the mirror with its copy at the same step. **Two version numbers**: `NET_VERSION` stays strict for lockstep and shared messages ([netplay.js:897-898](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L897-L898), [api.js:271-273](../server/api.js#L271-L273)), and `VIEW_VERSION` moves only when the view format does, as Minecraft's protocol number moves only when its wire does ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Protocol_version)). **Canary worlds**: one ordinary world on the owner's server, labelled experimental, never wiped (rolled back to a backup at worst), with a hero rule that cannot write back to a device. **Announced restarts**: today the updater installs at 3 am "never while players are in" ([updater.js:219-222](../server/update/updater.js#L219-L222)), which starves a world that never empties, so `6C-3` adds a countdown, a drain and an automatic way back in, as Old School RuneScape warns before every update ([OSRS Wiki](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/System_updates)).

Switches are inventory with a carrying cost. Release toggles should live "a week or two" and each should have a removal task or an expiry that fails a test ([Hodgson, 2017](https://martinfowler.com/articles/feature-toggles.html)); Knight Capital lost about $440 million in 45 minutes when a reused flag woke dead code on one server that had missed a deploy ([Statsig's account](https://statsig.com/blog/lose-half-a-billion-dollars-with-bad-feature-flags-knight-capital)). The plan's standing rules, written into `CLAUDE.md` and `contribution.md` by `F-1`, follow from that: no message kind or setting name is ever reused; every switch has an expiry test; a change to what is replicated updates the op path and the view schema together; **no replication pull request bumps `SNAPSHOT_VERSION`**, so a release can always be rolled back without stranding a save (an older server refuses a newer save, [host.js:3304-3330](../client/js/core/host.js#L3304-L3330)); and a transport change is tested in both modes. The release rollback itself is the updater's own (the previous version kept, backups before the update, [SERVER.md:448-462](../docs/SERVER.md#L448-L462)) and is rehearsed once per gate.

### Testing two netcodes without doubling CI

CI today is fifteen jobs, each given about 19 minutes of work: `test` (lint and 319 unit files), `motion`, `e2e (1 of 12)` to `(12 of 12)`, and `ci`, which runs nothing of its own and passes only if every other job did ([ci.yml:136-147](../.github/workflows/ci.yml#L136-L147), [ci.yml:175-179](../.github/workflows/ci.yml#L175-L179), [contribution.md:158-163](../contribution.md#L158-L163)). The 343 browser tests have been drawn cheaply since #695 and come to 234 minutes one after another, where they were 379; they are split fourteen ways by measured duration ([e2e-shard.js:1-14](../scripts/e2e-shard.js#L1-L14)), a share for each `e2e` job and one each for `motion` and `test` after their own work. The twelve-job layout has not been timed over enough runs to say when a run ends; the first cheaply drawn run, still on 8 `e2e` jobs, ended 36 minutes in. The `main` ruleset needs a pull request, an up-to-date branch and the one check `ci`, with no bypass and no merge queue ([contribution.md:684-707](../contribution.md#L684-L707)), and CI no longer runs on `main` after a merge; `soak.yml` and `mac.yml` are not required. A job added to `ci.yml` goes into `ci`'s `needs` and changes no setting. A required check whose workflow is skipped by a path filter stays pending and blocks the merge ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/merge-and-close-pull-requests/troubleshooting-required-status-checks)), so no path-filtered workflow may ever be made required.

The strategy keeps the pull-request path at half an hour. **Most replication tests run in Node**, where the view builder, the mirror and the comparator are pure modules like `netplay.js`. A helper in `test/helpers.js` attaches a mirror to each player of any `Host` scenario and asserts no mismatch; applied to the ten scenario files that already use `Host.checksum()` ([host.js:3496](../client/js/core/host.js#L3496)), it turns ambushes, invasions and trades into replication tests for seconds of CI time. A Node harness after [joinedgame.js:1-7](../test/joinedgame.js#L1-L7) plays host, view encoder, link, mirror and forecast in one process over the bad-link kit. **Only about six browser tests run in both modes** (join, walk and compare, takeover on another device, rejoin after reload, join mid-invasion, a server world's enter), following the rule to test what is going live plus the fallback and not every combination ([Hodgson, 2017](https://martinfowler.com/articles/feature-toggles.html)); the other multi-client tests run in lockstep until gate (b) and replicated after it. That adds about 10 to 15 minutes of serial time to 234 (estimate), about a minute a job; if the jobs outgrow their share, `E2E_AHEAD` is re-balanced or a thirteenth `e2e` job is added, which since #696 is two numbers in `ci.yml` and no repository setting. **Scale soaks run nightly** in a new `scale.yml` outside `ci.yml`. **Budgets are tests**: on pull requests they assert repeatable counts, and time is left to the nightly run and the Mac.

### Observability comes first, because every gate is a measurement

Today the server measures step times and keeps no history: `health()` reports each world's recent mean, p99 and worst with memory and players and, since #697, the whole tick's times, the world's pace and the time it has let go ([manage.js:1552-1596](../server/manage.js#L1552-L1596)), journals are free-text lines, and crash reports are seen on the management page alone. The plan builds a small dependency-free metrics registry before Stage 1 (`6A-1` to `6A-5`, in Stage 0's table) and adds alerts, client beacons and an operations document before gate (b) (`6A-6` to `6A-8`). `soak:server` writes the same histograms into its report, so the gates of Stages 1 to 4 and the live server's charts read one source. OpenTelemetry and metrics client libraries are left out because each would be a runtime dependency for a server that has none; anything that speaks Prometheus can scrape `/metrics` later, and Grafana Cloud's free tier (10,000 series, no card: [Grafana](https://grafana.com/pricing/?pg=pricing)) stays an optional recipe.

One repository rule runs through every table that follows. A slice that adds a script, a workflow, a version rule or a setting carries its `contribution.md` and `docs/SERVER.md` changes in the same pull request, as `CLAUDE.md` requires, and `test/contribution.test.js` fails if an npm script or a workflow is not named there ([contribution.test.js:29-41](../test/contribution.test.js#L29-L41)). A slice that adds or changes a sound carries its `client/js/audio/catalog.js` entry.

### The Mac and a rented Linux server as equals

Linux is already the platform CI tests ([ci.yml:47](../.github/workflows/ci.yml#L47)) and already has a platform module ([linux.js:1-6](../server/platform/linux.js#L1-L6)); what it lacks is packaging and an install-and-update test, which only the Mac has ([contribution.md:218-246](../contribution.md#L218-L246)). The plan therefore holds four rules. Every design choice must run unchanged on both: this is why Stage 4 keeps sockets on a gateway thread, since Node's `reusePort` raises an error on macOS ([Node 22 net](https://nodejs.org/docs/latest-v22.x/api/net.html)). Every operational tool (metrics, charts, alerts, backups, the drain) is built into the server with no platform service behind it. Every gate's rollback is rehearsed on both. And thread counts are computed per platform, P cores only on Apple silicon. A rented server is managed through the existing loopback page over an SSH tunnel, so no admin route ever faces the internet ([admin.js:1-17](../server/admin.js#L1-L17)).

## Stage 0 measures the authority, the phone and the home line first

**Method, and who ships it.** Two instruments, as the industry uses. CCP load-tests EVE with thin clients against a real node and found most time in the pre-tick (deciding which clients to inform and serialising each update), under 10% in physics ([CCP, 2010](https://www.eveonline.com/article/fixing-lag-drakes-of-destiny-part-1-1)); Albion runs bots beside its simulation for large battles ([Unity, 2026](https://unity.com/blog/albion-online-cross-platform-pvp-mmo-architecture)); Colyseus warns that a load tester in one process "can hit its limit before your server does" ([Colyseus docs](https://docs.colyseus.io/tools/loadtest)). So `npm run load` is an in-process, exactly repeatable harness that includes the flush and encode, and `soak:server` gains thin bots that hold no world copy. Metrics use what Node has built in (`createHistogram`, `monitorEventLoopDelay`, `eventLoopUtilization`, GC entries: [Node perf_hooks](https://nodejs.org/docs/latest-v24.x/api/perf_hooks.html)) and the Prometheus text format, which is a page of code ([Prometheus](https://prometheus.io/docs/instrumenting/exposition_formats/)). **Method, and who ships it.** A flat grid keyed by integer cell fixes every pass: the standard advice is to prefer it over hierarchies and to move an object between cells only when it crosses a border ([Game Programming Patterns](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/spatial-partition.html)), and Epic built Replication Graph on grid cells because per-actor, per-connection relevance bottlenecked at "100 connected players and about 50,000 replicated Actors" ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/replication-graph-in-unreal-engine)). The determinism rule that keeps lockstep safe is the stage's core idea: **use the grid only to cull candidates, then run the unchanged predicate over them in the same canonical order**, so results are bit-identical, checksums agree and no `NET_VERSION` bump is needed. Four rules follow. Derived indexes are never saved and never depend on history. The predicate is kept (replacing `hypot(dx, dy) <= R` by a squared test is deterministic but not equal at the boundary, so it is a rule change). The order of side effects is kept. And a cell's list order never reaches an outcome. A static `Host.culling` switch that makes every query return everyone proves equivalence in a test, as the battle's indexed and unindexed play already is proven equal ([field-battle.test.js:105-121](../test/field-battle.test.js#L105-L121)). For overload, EVE slows a node's clock "dynamically and in very fine increments" rather than fall behind ([CCP, 2011](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/introducing-time-dilation-tidi)), and capping steps per tick is the classic cure for the "spiral of death" ([Fiedler, 2004](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)); lockstep clients already tolerate a slow authority, so the fix is to stop catching up. For joins, Factorio caps simultaneous map downloads and no longer slows the game for a slow client ([Factorio wiki](https://wiki.factorio.com/Multiplayer)); its slot queue once blocked joins with slots free ([0.17.32 note](https://devtrackers.gg/factorio/p/6cb623c4-version-0-17-32-released)), which is the bug class the tests target. A worker cannot take the snapshot encode cheaply because `postMessage` clones the object synchronously ([Node worker_threads](https://nodejs.org/docs/latest-v22.x/api/worker_threads.html)), so snapshots are made less often and cheaper in place; V8's fast `JSON.stringify` path also requires no replacer ([V8 blog, 2025](https://v8.dev/blog/json-stringify)).

## Stage 2 makes strangers safe to share a world, proven at eight

**Goal.** Give a world shared by strangers its rules, each a key in the world's `RULES` that defaults to today's behaviour, set together by a `shared` preset that a `public` world must use. **Gate** (adjusted to decision 3). An 8-bot `soak:server` scenario under the `shared` preset (two parties of four, a town, a road, two runs of one dungeon site at once, one rift, one invasion) for 2 h on the Mac: every minute's p99 ≤ 50 ms, memory within 15% of its level an hour in, at most one resync per player-hour, and the rule assertions hold (live director things never over the area cap; a bystander is paid nothing; loot per head and time to kill inside a band; two runs' maps disjoint). `npm run load` at 25 hostless players with the rules on gives authority-only evidence (mean ≤ 25 ms, p99 ≤ 50 ms) without opening any world above 8. **The owner's rules changes of 11 November 2026.** Pull request #697 changed several rules on the owner's word or with the owner told, all in `NET_VERSION` 224, and most of them touch this stage. The first raid comes ten minutes into a character's time in towns, not five ([invasions.js:62-74](../client/js/core/invasions.js#L62-L74)); ten minutes is also when an idle player is marked away, and an away player's time is not counted, so a player idle since they began meets it 30 seconds after they are back, and `2-8`'s marks should keep that. The cap on who goes for a player (6 up close and 4 shooting, `AGGRO`) now holds back only those the player has not struck ([GAME.md:1109-1118](../docs/GAME.md#L1109-L1118)); it is the guards' and soldiers' cap, counted for each player, and the wild's creatures have none, so 25 players together can have 250 on them (arithmetic), and `2-16`'s curve should be read with it. A follower whose leader is down or on another floor now defends itself and otherwise holds where it is, which is the behaviour `2-21` and `4-3` meet when a hero goes through a door without their company. A stun on a boss lasts half as long and each within 20 s of the last half as long again (`STUNS`), which already blunts the stun-lock a crowd would bring to the leaders of `2-14` and `2-16`. Of the rest, a shot now gives up past one and a half times its reach; what burns on the ground passes no one as its striker once its caster has fallen, so `2-11`'s ledger credits no one for that harm unless it says otherwise; and guards and beasts set on no one look round four times a second, not every step. **Method, and who ships it.** World of Warcraft gives each group a copy of a dungeon and caps new instances at ten an hour per account ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Instance)); Guild Wars 2 removed the instance owner in 2014, lets players join a run in progress and pays the bonus on the first completion each day ([GW2 Wiki: Dungeon](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Dungeon)). Guild Wars 2 credits a kill past about 5% to 10% of the enemy's health and says a player "must still be actively attacking targets to receive rewards" ([GW2 Wiki: Loot](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Loot)); Throne and Liberty ranks boss contribution by damage and healing with a minimum to be eligible ([Throne and Liberty](https://www.playthroneandliberty.com/en-ca/news/articles/loot-distribution-rules)). Minecraft's `players_sleeping_percentage` is the shipped form of rest by share ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Game_rule)); Guild Wars 2's squads ([GW2 Wiki: Squad](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Squad)) and World of Warcraft's `/say` heard within 60 yards ([Warcraft Wiki: Chat](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Chat)) are the models for groups above a party and for chat by distance. The governing design rule is Koster's: instance the "embedded games", keep the commons common ([Koster, 2005](https://www.raphkoster.com/2005/11/30/from-instancing-to-worldy-games/)). Every rule lives in `Host`, so all of it carries to replication unchanged, and a dungeon run is the unit Stage 4 moves to a thread.

The notes recommended no player against player at all, so they did not research duels or a war flag. Slices `2-3` and `2-4` therefore carry this plan's own proposal, which the owner should confirm when `2-2` merges. A duel is asked and accepted like a party invitation, makes the two heroes and their companies hostile to each other alone, ends when one yields, falls to a health floor, goes 60 m away or changes map, and pays no loot, tag, skill or death. The war flag is set per character in a settlement, takes effect after a delay and clears only out of a fight; flagged players of peoples at war are enemies, and an unflagged player is never a valid target. Area spells already strike only the hostile ([battle.js:5902-5924](../client/js/core/battle.js#L5902-L5924)), so neither needs a friendly-fire rule.

**Risks.** Lockstep cost grows with every occupied dungeon level, so the live-run cap is real until Stage 3. A cheap reset becomes the best farm, hence the hourly cap and the daily hoard. Tag thresholds set too high punish tanks and healers, hence harm taken and healing in the ledger; set too low, one arrow is a share, hence tiers on leaders only. With rest personal, night-only content needs a day short enough to wait for; a day is 60 minutes of play, which the owner should judge in P1. A recurring unique loses its "only once" feel, which is the price of fairness to the hundredth player. A run must hold no reference into overworld state beyond its door and its owner, so that Stage 4 can hand it to a thread.

## Stage 6, brought forward: identity, moderation, law and operations before any stranger above 8

**Goal.** Everything a world needs before strangers can find and join it above 8 players: an identity that bans can hold to, moderation that works while the one moderator sleeps, the legal minimum, a restricted chat mode for countries that limit children's chat with strangers (decision 12), a hidden home address, alerts, off-site backups with a drill, and restarts that do not strand players. **Gate.** This track has no gate of its own; it is the larger half of gate (b). In code, `publicReady()` is true only when `6B-1` to `6B-14` are merged and switched on, Nanny Mode is on with a country source no older than 62 days and a country list reviewed in the last 90 days (`2-36`, `2-37`, `6B-16` to `6B-23` and `6B-25`), a contact address is set, the front is not a direct port, and the retention job ran in the last day. The owner's checklist completes it: the three Ofcom assessments written and dated, the privacy notice and rules published, the lawyer's review taken or waived (A1), and one rehearsal in which a second device is reported, muted, banned and tries to return with a new identity, and in which a device in Nanny Mode is shown to send and receive quick phrases only. Under decision 3 the slices are spread across phases 3 to 5; every one defaults off, so private worlds and browser-hosted games behave as today at each merge.

**What exists today.** Pellagos already has an account without an email: a device-held Ed25519 key ([you.js:2](../client/js/app/you.js#L2)), an id derived from it ([player.js:42-57](../client/js/core/player.js#L42-L57)), a 54-character code that carries the identity to another device ([player.js:66-105](../client/js/core/player.js#L66-L105)), and a signed entry proof accepted once within ten minutes ([api.js:156-180](../server/api.js#L156-L180)). Owners can kick, ban and set roles ([api.js:633-638](../server/api.js#L633-L638)), and every check passes through four policy hooks written so that other identity schemes can plug in ([policy.js:1-10](../server/access/policy.js#L1-L10)). What is missing is anything server-wide (identity and bans are recorded per world, in each world's `access.json`: [world.js:1-8](../server/access/world.js#L1-L8)) and anything about speech: there is no mute, report, chat log or filter, blocking is in the blocker's own game only ([netplay.js:45-46](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L45-L46)), and a hero's name is only trimmed to 20 characters ([netplay.js:377](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L377)). Cheating by speed or teleport is already impossible, because the client sends orders and the authority simulates them ([host.js:2178-2188](../client/js/core/host.js#L2178-L2188)); the weak points are that every lockstep client holds the whole world, that a `visiting` hero is written by the client, and that the play socket has one coarse bucket of 1,200 messages a minute, beside which `Hosting` itself has held each joined game since #697 to 30 commands a second, 64 KB a message and the world again once every 10 s, however it is linked (`HEARD`, `AGAIN`: [netplay.js:134-153](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L134-L153)) ([play.js:68](../server/play.js#L68), [limits.js:28](../server/access/limits.js#L28)). Tailscale Funnel, driven by the server today ([funnel.js:1-15](../server/funnel.js#L1-L15)), already hides the home address; a directly forwarded port with a DuckDNS name publishes it ([SERVER.md:277-293](../docs/SERVER.md#L277-L293)). All backups sit on the same disk as the data ([worlds.js:42](../server/storage/worlds.js#L42), [worlds.js:61](../server/storage/worlds.js#L61)); no message warns of a restart; and names, addresses and crash reports are kept with no retention limit ([crashes.js:1-10](../server/storage/crashes.js#L1-L10)).

**Method, and who ships it.** The device key stays the account, and a new identity is made free to create but slow to become useful, which is how RuneScape gates new free accounts: by hours played, not by identity ([OSRS Wiki](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Account)). Moderation follows RuneScape's volunteer model in miniature: a mute that acts at once and is short (cut from 48 hours to one in 2014), reviewed afterwards ([RuneScape Wiki](https://runescape.wiki/w/Player_Moderator)). The owner's decision for typed chat accepts a known risk with open eyes: Spry Fox reports that "the vast majority of online toxicity shows up" in open communication with strangers ([Spry Fox, 2025](https://spryfox.com/2025/09/17/the-challenge-of-open-communication-in-online-games/)), and Ofcom's games guidance names text chat as a route used for grooming ([Lewis Silkin on Ofcom, 2025](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/10/24/ofcom-issues-guidance-about-online-safety-act-in-video-gaming-context-102lr5v)). So the tools that contain it are conditions of opening, and unattended defaults do the work: three distinct reporters in ten minutes silence a player's public channels until reviewed, and one switch closes every public world. In front of the server, Funnel's visitors reach a relay that "cannot decrypt data" and never learn the device's address, under "non-configurable bandwidth limits" that are unpublished ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/docs/features/tailscale-funnel)); Cloudflare supports WebSockets on all plans, warns that it "may restart servers, which terminates WebSockets connections" ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/network/websockets/)), and its Tunnel is outbound-only but needs a domain ([Tunnel docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/networks/connectors/cloudflare-tunnel/)). Alerts need no service: an outbound heartbeat to a dead-man's switch covers power, network and a hung process from a home Mac with no inbound port ([healthchecks.io](https://healthchecks.io/pricing/) watches 20 jobs free), and ntfy publishes with a plain HTTP `POST` ([ntfy docs](https://docs.ntfy.sh/)).

### Nanny Mode: quick phrases only for listed countries, until an age check passes

**What it is.** Nanny Mode is a state of one player's chat. A player in it sends and sees pre-canned quick phrases and nothing typed: typed messages from other players do not reach them, and they cannot type to others. Everything else in the game is as it was, and typed chat stays open to every player who is not in it. The owner's instruction covers what reaches such a player; limiting what they send as well is a default, taken because the rules concern contact in both directions, because a child could otherwise type a phone number to a stranger, and because a conversation whose replies cannot be read looks broken. The server works the state out when a player enters and enforces it where every chat line already passes, `Hosting.#say`, which sends one encoded string to each recipient in a loop ([netplay.js:834-884](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L834-L884)); the receiving client repeats the check. It covers every player-to-player channel (`say`, `area`, party, squad and both kinds of direct message), because a party on a public world can be four strangers. The game's own lines and heroes' names still arrive; names are written by players, so the name filter of `6B-8` is the control there.

**Who is in it, and why.** There are three ways in. The first is the country of the connection. The server keeps a list, `nannyCountries`, of countries whose rules limit children's chat with strangers, each entry with an age threshold, a reason, a source and a `reviewed` date; the default list shipped with the game is the United Kingdom at 16, Brazil at 18 and Indonesia at 16, and the legal section below gives the reason for each. An account seen from a listed country stays flagged for 30 days after the last sighting, so that switching on a VPN does not lift it at once. The second way in is an unknown country (no match, Tor, no country file, or a file more than 62 days old), because a missing file must not be the way out. The third is the player's own choice, with an optional lock code for a parent. That is a real parental control, and it also keeps the icon from meaning "this player is in the United Kingdom" or "this player is a child".

Today the server knows each connection's address and no country ([guards.js:81-84](../server/guards.js#L81-L84)). Where Cloudflare is in front, its `CF-IPCountry` header supplies one ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/reference/http-headers/)); otherwise the server reads DB-IP's free country file, which is CC BY 4.0, needs no account and comes monthly as a 4.6 MB download ([DB-IP](https://db-ip.com/db/download/ip-to-country-lite)), held in about 10 MB of memory (estimate). The server downloads it into its data folder and the repository never holds it; "IP Geolocation by DB-IP" is credited in the game's Credits, on the management page and in the Nanny Mode explanation. A country header on a direct connection is never trusted.

**Where it applies is a default the owner should rule on.** The server setting is `nanny: "off" | "public" | "all"`. The plan's default is `public`: Nanny Mode applies on public worlds, where strangers meet, on the reasoning that a private world is entered with a key from someone the player knows, much as Xbox leaves unverified UK accounts "limited to friends only" and not silent ([Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/07/28/xbox-age-verification-uk/)). **The owner's words do not contain that limit: read literally, chat to those addresses "must be limited" on every world, which is the setting `all`.** The difference is one setting, and the lawyer is asked whether a keyed world counts as known users under the UK text (A1). The player's own switch works on every world whatever the setting says.

**What each side sees.** The owner asked that Nanny Mode, its icon and its removal be well described in the chat panel, so the wording is part of the design. The table is the draft. Lines marked † are this plan's additions to the note's draft, for the two cases it left unworded.

The icon is a speech bubble under an umbrella, with no child imagery. It appears beside each Nanny Mode player's name in the chat log, the "To" picker and the Players tab, and nowhere outside the chat panel: never over a hero, never on a map. **How it is removed.** The country and unknown-country forms lift only on a passed age verification at or above the entry's threshold. A self-declared age never lifts them. The player's own form lifts by switching it off, with the lock code if one was set. At 16, Ofcom's assessment of 29 October 2026 accepts photo-ID matching, digital identity services, banking-based checks and facial age estimation (the weakest, with a suggested challenge age of 19); it finds that credit card, mobile operator and email methods "cannot currently reliably distinguish 16- and 17-year-olds from younger children" and that parental consent is "insufficiently robust at 16" ([Osborne Clarke, 2 November 2026](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/uk-online-safety-ofcom-assesses-highly-effective-age-assurance-methods-online-services)). The design is neutral between providers: the server starts a session with the provider, the player goes to the provider's own page, and the server polls for the answer, which suits a home Mac with no inbound webhook. The game asks only "over N", never sees a photo or a document, and keeps four fields: passed, threshold, date and provider. A failed or under-age answer leaves Nanny Mode on with everything else playable, stores only a count of attempts, and allows three attempts in 30 days so that an adult misread by a face estimate can use another method. A pass lasts for good and lifts Nanny Mode within a minute on every world of the server; because the device key can be copied to another device ([player.js:66-105](../client/js/core/player.js#L66-L105)), a moderator has a "check again". **No provider is switched on until the owner approves its cost (A4). Until then the panel says plainly that age verification is not offered on this server yet, and Nanny Mode cannot be lifted there.**

**Who ships something like it.** Roblox has required an age check to chat everywhere since 7 January 2026 ([Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/01/roblox-age-checks-required-to-chat)). Xbox has limited unverified UK accounts to friends since 28 July 2025, with purchases untouched ([Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/07/28/xbox-age-verification-uk/)). Epic's cabined accounts leave under-13s in Rocket League with preset messages only ([Epic help](https://www.epicgames.com/help/rocket-league-c-202300000001622/a202300000016831)), and RuneScape limits under-13 accounts to Quick Chat ([RuneScape Wiki](https://runescape.wiki/w/Quick_Chat)). They name the state plainly, leave play untouched and lift it with one check. **None found puts a visible mark on other players**, so that part of the owner's requirement has no precedent to learn from.

**Browser-hosted games.** There the host's browser is the authority and the server never sees chat: the relay passes messages "untouched" ([relay.js:1-5](../server/relay.js#L1-L5)) and the broker "reads nothing of what's said but who it's for" ([broker.js:1-5](../server/broker.js#L1-L5)). So the receiving client drops typed lines when its own Nanny Mode is on, and the broker tells a client its state on connecting (`6B-25`). A modified client can ignore that, and the documentation says so. Because strangers can already find browser-hosted games through the broker's list, `6B-25` need not wait for gate (b).

**Risks particular to Nanny Mode.** A visible icon could mark a likely child to exactly the people the rules mean to keep away. Four things reduce that: any player may switch Nanny Mode on, so the icon does not mean "in the United Kingdom" or "a child"; the label names the capability and not the person, and the explanation says it "says nothing else about them"; the icon has no child imagery and appears only in the chat panel; and a Nanny Mode player's whereabouts are hidden from players outside their party. Under lockstep that hiding is in the panel only, since every client holds the whole world; replication makes it real (`3-29`). Because no precedent puts such a mark on other players, this part is untested anywhere and is watched through the report reason of `6B-23`. A VPN defeats the country lookup. The government has said "We will not ban or age-gate VPNs" while in-scope services "must take robust steps to prevent, detect and respond to underage circumvention" ([government response, 15 July 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a57d7ce31fb6daf31413797/NCP_Government_response_A.pdf)); the 30-day hold and the report reason are the plan's steps. Country lookup errs: vendors' claims of 99% at country level are disputed, accuracy varies between "cellular vs. broadband, IPv4 vs. IPv6" ([MaxMind](https://support.maxmind.com/hc/en-us/articles/4407630607131)), and a roaming phone usually exits in its home country, so some adults will be restricted wrongly and some players missed. **No age check was found that is both free to the operator and sound at 16**, so until A4 is approved a player in a listed country cannot leave Nanny Mode at all; one user-shared graph had Roblox's chat use falling from about 90% to 36.5% after its checks ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/412073)), and Discord delayed and narrowed its own scheme after a backlash over privacy ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/24/discord-delays-global-rollout-of-age-verification-after-backlash/)). The UK rule's text is unpublished, so the design may prove too wide (a party of friends) or too narrow (private worlds). An adult can pass the check and hand the code to a child, which the moderator's "check again" answers only after the fact. And in browser-hosted games the filter runs in the receiver's own client.

### What typed chat, and Nanny Mode, bring in law

This is not legal advice. It sets out what the research found in official and law-firm sources, marks what is uncertain, and makes a lawyer's review a priced approval point (A1).

**Being small exempts almost nothing, and nothing here charges a fee.** The UK Online Safety Act reaches a service that "has a significant number of United Kingdom users", targets the UK, or can be used there with "reasonable grounds to believe that there is a material risk of significant harm" ([OSA s.4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/4)); its user-generated content is anything "generated directly on the service by a user" that "may be encountered by another user", with no exclusion for chat or names ([OSA s.55](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/55)). Fees fall only on providers above a worldwide revenue threshold ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/online-safety-fees-and-penalties)), but enforcement has reached small overseas services: 4chan was fined £20,000 in August 2025 and £520,000 in March 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Act_2023)).

Three further points bound the uncertainty. And the research could not open Ofcom's own games guidance or toolkit pages (they returned 403), so their content here comes from law-firm summaries; US state laws were not analysed. Because the age screen, the chat channels, the listing, and Nanny Mode's country list, thresholds and scope are settings and not code paths, a change in the rules is a change of configuration.

**Risks.** One moderator sleeps, so the automatic mute and the "public worlds off" switch must be safe unattended. A key is free to remake, so the aim is that evasion costs hours, not that it is impossible. Shared addresses (carrier NAT, schools) make prefix bans blunt, so they stay short. Local storage can be cleared, and with it the account; the saved code is the only recovery. Funnel may throttle or change terms without notice, and a public server's name is hard to change once shared, so the front is chosen before the first listing. After a power cut a Mac's launch agent starts only once someone logs in; the heartbeat reports that within two minutes. Nanny Mode's own risks are set out in its subsection above.

**The owner's actions.** Approvals A1 to A5 and the paperwork of A10. Rule on Nanny Mode's scope, `public` or `all`. Ask Yoti, k-ID and VerifyMy for quotes at once, so that A4 can be decided when the UK regulations and Ofcom's guidance for 16 are final. Put in the diary the UK regulations (the first set is due by 31 December 2026), Brazil's enforcement start (January 2027 by one firm's account) and the UAE's deadline of 1 January 2027, and review the country list every 90 days. Choose the contact alias and the alert channel, and receive each alert once by hand. Apply the documented power settings on the Mac. Run the rehearsal with a second device: moderation, and Nanny Mode by the test setting and by the player's own switch.

## Stage 3 replaces the feed, not the game: seams, dark views, shadow, canary

**Goal.** The server keeps running the same `Host` and sends each client a view of what is near it; the client keeps a partial mirror for the renderer and the HUD, predicts only its own hero and draws everyone else slightly in the past. **Gate** (the stage's exit, at the 50 tier). Fifty spread mirror bots for 2 h on the Mac: ≤ 20 KB/s mean and ≤ 30 KB/s in a client's worst second; replication ≤ 3 ms mean and ≤ 8 ms p99 of the step; step p99 ≤ 50 ms; upload ≤ 12 Mbit/s; heap flat; a client beside a 400-actor battle ≤ 30 KB/s with its nearest 32 actors at 20 Hz. In the Node harness at a 300 ms round trip with 40 ms jitter, a remote actor is drawn within 0.3 m (95th percentile) of where the server had it at playout time. A page hidden 30 s is right within 1 s of being shown, and a socket killed while hidden is replaced within 5 s without a reload. The owner's phone meets the frame threshold at 25 and at 50 bots (P6).

**What exists today.** A joiner gets one full snapshot (0.45 to 4.5 MB in the earlier audit; since #697, which keeps each character at about 0.8 KB where it was 2, 80 KB of text in a start town and 310 KB for eight players, sent compressed at a sixth to a fifth of that) and then a JSON op stream it replays ([netplay.js:595-618](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L595-L618)); nothing is filtered per recipient except chat and `self`. The seeds of replication are already there: a 16-byte binary actor packer, whose stream has been off since `NET_VERSION` 224 unless a host sets `sendsPlaces` ([motion.js:8-14](../client/js/core/motion.js#L8-L14)); a per-player state message that acknowledges commands by sequence number ([netplay.js:695-711](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L695-L711), [selfstate.js:16](../client/js/core/selfstate.js#L16)); commands applied on arrival and simulated, never a client-stated position ([netplay.js:1007-1022](../client/js/core/netplay.js#L1007-L1022)); and one place where every event leaves the simulation, the return value of `Host.advance()` ([host.js:2935-2941](../client/js/core/host.js#L2935-L2941)), about 60 battle kinds and 118 host kinds. The server's socket is text only, and since #697 it compresses what it sends over 1 KB for a browser that offers permessage-deflate, with a `compress` setting: an inbound binary frame closes it with 1003 ([relay.js:391-392](../server/relay.js#L391-L392)). On the client the dependence on a whole world is narrow in files and wide in call sites: `game.js` has 829 lines that read `host.` or `battle.` and calls `battle.actor(` 309 times, while the HUD, minimap, maps and panels are handed views by it. A joined game builds the world from the seed and restores the whole snapshot into a `Host` ([session.js:94-95](../client/js/app/session.js#L94-L95)), and the frame loop replays the host's steps in `#tick` ([game.js:4213](../client/js/app/game.js#L4213)). About half the client's systems already work on what is within 160 m; avatars are made and removed by comparing `battle.actors` with the avatars held ([game.js:2989-3039](../client/js/app/game.js#L2989-L3039)), so the renderer needs no new concept for actors entering and leaving. **The shipped forecast already is client-side prediction with server reconciliation**: it resets a private battle to the host's latest word of the hero and replays unacknowledged commands ([forecast.js:227-253](../client/js/app/forecast.js#L227-L253)), and when it shipped, distance jumped fell from 1.7 to 24 m per scenario to 0.05 to 2.2 m ([hero_prediction_plan.md](../generated/hero_prediction_plan.md)). The client also already builds land only round its own player; what makes a joined copy build the whole populated map is the replay, so removing the replay solves terrain with no new terrain code.

**Method, and who ships it.** On the server, each client has a *known set* of actors, refreshed every fifth step and staggered across clients: entered within 160 m on the same map, left beyond 192 m, with a short pinned list (the viewer's company and party, its target, anyone targeting it, quest actors) and a cap of 256, pinned first and hostiles before neutrals, so that Guild Wars 2's old fault of invisible nearby enemies under server-side culling cannot happen inside the cap ([GW2 Wiki: Culling](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Culling)). One binary frame per step per client carries field-masked records chosen by a priority accumulator under a byte budget, the reference design ([Gaffer on Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/state_synchronization/)) that Unity's Netcode for Entities also ships ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode@1.5/manual/optimizations.html)). **Positions are absolute centimetres inside a 512 m region, never arithmetic deltas**, so a record is the same for every viewer and never depends on the one before; that avoids by construction the Minecraft bug in which deltas were taken against the server's tracker rather than what a late-joining player had been sent ([Mojang MC-170907](https://bugs.mojang.com/browse/MC-170907)). A walking actor costs 8 bytes; a full view all moving is about 12 KB/s and a typical one about 20 KB/s with events and the player's own state (arithmetic that `3-14` confirms); a token bucket caps a frame at 1,500 bytes. Over an ordered transport the baseline is simply "what I last sent you", as in Colyseus, which ships on WebSocket ([Colyseus](https://docs.colyseus.io/state)), so acknowledged baselines are unnecessary; what must be built by hand is dropping, which on TCP means not writing state for a congested client and later sending only the newest values. WebTransport is not ready at either end: a WebKit bug reports its datagrams undefined on iOS ([WebKit 322201](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=322201)) and Node's QUIC is "early development" ([Node 26 docs](https://nodejs.org/download/release/v26.11.0/docs/api/quic.md)); the frame is datagram-safe, so a later move changes the transport only. Events are not given a second producer: a declarative table gives each kind an audience and a class (`must` or `may`), the server filters the one array per client, and the client feeds the result to the same handler it uses now; Riot found discrete events "100x to 10000x" cheaper than diffed properties in many cases ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/valorants-128-tick-servers)).

On the client the shape is branch by abstraction with two seams. **The mirror is a real `Battle` that is never stepped**, built the way the forecast's `fork` already builds a partial one ([battle.js:1819-1848](../client/js/core/battle.js#L1819-L1848)), so the 309 actor call sites, light, sight and targeting keep working; a thin world view with two suppliers stands in front of the `host` reads, and a hosting or solo page reads its own `Host` through it for good. Others are drawn from a ring of timed states about 100 ms behind the server, as Source renders 100 ms behind and extrapolates for at most 0.25 s ([Valve](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Source_Multiplayer_Networking)), with the delay sized from measured jitter as Photon Fusion does ([Fusion](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/v2/manual/advanced/time-synchronization)); an actor leaves only on an explicit message, never by silence ([Fusion 3](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion-core/v3/manual/interest-area)). The own hero follows the standard loop of applying inputs at once and re-running them from the server's state ([Gambetta](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/client-side-prediction-server-reconciliation.html)). Coming back from a hidden page never fast-forwards, because iOS can freeze a page before it is told ([WebKit 207256](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=207256)) and a socket opened after resume can hang ([WebKit 308073](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=308073)).

Shadow mode is the main safety device. A lockstep peer also asks for views; it holds each frame until its copy has played that step, then checks membership (every actor within 160 m or pinned is known, none past 192 m unless pinned, nothing hidden), values (position within 1 cm, heading within 1/256 turn, state and target equal, health within 1/255), staleness, events (the copy's events through the audience table equal the frame's `must` events and contain its `may` events, in order), the player's own state, and the byte budget. It cannot check feel, the partial-world client, or anything above 8 players; the phone checkpoints and the canary do that.

**Risks.** The read seam crosses a 20,817-line `game.js`, where merge conflicts with other contributors will come, so it goes early in many small pull requests. The world view is permanent: solo play and the hosting player read the authoritative `Host`, so Fowler's last step, removing the abstraction, does not apply. The host of a browser game has zero latency and no forecast, as Dead by Daylight's killer had ([Behaviour, 2019](https://devtrackers.gg/deadbydaylight/p/c8b0b94a-dedicated-servers-2-7-0-ptb-learnings)), so "feel parity" at gate (c) is judged from the joiner's seat. Fields the server never sends keep plausible defaults in the mirror; the strict reads of `3-16` turn that trap into a failing test. `battle.time` must mean playout time while the hero's own cooldowns need the predicted present. ## Stage 4 moves dungeons onto pooled threads through a two-phase door

**Goal.** Take dungeons, hells and Bellhaven's keep off the world thread as short-lived instances, so one world carries 100 players with combat. **Gate.** A hundred scripted players, spread, with combat, a field battle and an invasion, about 40 of them in ten instances, for 30 minutes: the world thread's and every instance thread's worst-minute p99 ≤ 50 ms; handoff p99 ≤ 150 ms; no hero lost or doubled; memory flat. It runs on the owner's Mac and on an 8-core Linux machine (A6); CI keeps a small in-process version inside the unit job.

**What exists today.** One Node process holds a supervisor that owns every socket and ends TLS, and one worker thread per world ([world-thread.js:36](../server/world-thread.js#L36)). Each player message crosses one `postMessage` each way, measured for the notes at about 3 to 6 µs on a 4-core Linux VM, so the hop is 1 to 2.5% of a core at 100 players (arithmetic) and is not the limit; the shared risk is the supervisor's single event loop. There is one `Battle` for every map and a door is a same-step teleport between maps ([battle.js:6116-6150](../client/js/core/battle.js#L6116-L6150)); dungeon levels are drawn far away at x = 20,000 ([insides.js:1313](../client/js/core/insides.js#L1313)). Making a dungeon's two or three levels took 41 to 163 ms in one step in the same measurements (since #697 a world server works the levels out on its layouts' worker once a player is within 150 m of the way in, so the step that makes the dungeon takes them ready-made), and with 8 players at 8 dungeon doors 227 of 311 actors were on inside maps with a worst step of 614 ms. `host.js` refers to `this.battle` on 681 lines, so giving a `Host` several battles is a rewrite, and a second, smaller `Host` per instance is the cheaper cut. The useful precedents are in place: a deferred far end of a link ([battle.js:6119-6122](../client/js/core/battle.js#L6119-L6122)), a place a hero returns to ([host.js:1869-1894](../client/js/core/host.js#L1869-L1894)), re-entry by token within five minutes ([play.js:186-217](../server/play.js#L186-L217)) and a registry that takes an injected `spawn` for tests ([registry.js:60](../server/registry.js#L60)). Each world also has three workers of its own since #697 (navigation, layouts and the land's heights), about 37 MB for the third, which `4-5`'s count of spare cores must allow for. Today's sizing counts all cores alike ([registry.js:41-47](../server/registry.js#L41-L47)).

**Method, and who ships it.** Shipped games hand a character off at a boundary the player already expects, through a durable record, with exactly one owner at a time. Albion hands players between game servers through the database even on one machine ([Salz, 2016](https://es.slideshare.net/davidsalz54/albion-online-software-architecture-of-an-mmo-talk-at-quo-vadis-2016-berlin)); New World hands state from hub to hub behind a few entry points that are the only public addresses, with session content on a separate pool ([AWS for Games](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/gametech/the-unique-architecture-behind-amazon-games-seamless-mmo-new-world)); Star Citizen's transit "was not designed for Server Meshing" and its stated bar is now "A player should never lose an item" ([StarShipDealers, 2026](https://www.starshipdealers.com/blog/sc-live-tech-talk-server-meshing-2026/)); SpacetimeDB's rule is an asynchronous message that results "in exactly one reducer call" on the target, never a blocking call ([SpacetimeDB](https://spacetimedb.com/blog/how-does-spacetime-scale)). Node fixes the socket design: worker threads cannot be handed sockets ([Node worker_threads](https://nodejs.org/docs/latest-v22.x/api/worker_threads.html)), so a gateway keeps them and forwards frames as transferred buffers, which also means a simulation thread can die without dropping its players. The design is an instance as a scoped `Host` on a pooled thread; a handoff of offer, take and done, coordinated by the supervisor with a per-hero epoch that fences stale messages; a timeout that always puts the hero back outside the door; inputs that arrive in transit dropped; and no step ever waiting on another thread. Building insides stay on the world thread: a taproom has 7 actors and is tied to its town through stock, hiring and quests. **Overworld regions are not built**: every shipped seamless split paid for its borders for years, and in Folia data shared between regions can be corrupted and parts of the API are unsupported ([PaperMC/Folia](https://github.com/PaperMC/Folia)). On the M4 Pro only performance cores count, because a thread that spills to an efficiency core runs at roughly 57% of the clock ([Eclectic Light, 2024](https://eclecticlight.co/2024/12/05/inside-m4-chips-cpu-core-management/)).

## Stage 6 proper: SQLite records and a ledger close the duplication windows

**Goal.** Per-character records and a trade ledger in one transactional store, world state written behind, and a tested way back at every step. **Gate.** The restore drills pass in CI and weekly on the live server; the duplication suite passes; a drained restart returns 25 bots within 30 s at the 95th percentile; a week of dual write shows no mismatch; the recovery point is at most an hour off-site and 30 s on a process crash. The ledger half (`6D-1` to `6D-4`) is due at gate (b); the cut-over follows replication and precedes the 100 tier.

**What exists today.** Everything is a file written whole and atomically ([files.js:63-84](../server/storage/files.js#L63-L84)). A world is one snapshot a minute, encoded synchronously on the world's thread ([worlds.js:286-293](../server/storage/worlds.js#L286-L293)); a kept hero is one JSON file written only when its player leaves ([world-worker.js:150-155](../server/world-worker.js#L150-L155)). A trade is atomic in memory ([host.js:5262-5292](../client/js/core/host.js#L5262-L5292)) and since #697 an agreeing names the offer it saw (`rev`), so a changed offer is refused as `reoffered`; but nothing is written at the swap, items have no identity ([progress.js:490](../client/js/core/progress.js#L490)), and reopening a world re-keeps heroes from the snapshot without asking which is newer ([world-worker.js:88-99](../server/world-worker.js#L88-L99)). Read from the code, none reproduced, five windows follow: a crash inside the minute after one trader has left; an autosave restore that rolls the world back under today's hero files ([manage.js:652-655](../server/manage.js#L652-L655)); ground items and chests picked up by someone who then leaves; a device-held character re-imported in `visiting` and `copies` worlds; and a leave racing a rejoin.

**Method, and who ships it.** The proven shape is one conditional update with a double-entry ledger row in the same transaction, so that total supply is a one-line invariant ([DEV write-up](https://dev.to/kaviyakumar23/how-i-made-item-duplication-impossible-to-represent-in-a-game-economy-with-aurora-dsql-46pg), a hobby project). The cost of not having it is on record: New World disabled all wealth transfers in November 2021 over a gold duplication ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/amazon-disables-new-world-wealth-transfers-to-fight-gold-dupe-exploit)). Node's built-in `node:sqlite` is a release candidate from Node 24.15 and is synchronous ([Node 24 docs](https://nodejs.org/docs/latest-v24.x/api/sqlite.html)), so a commit finishes before the step's messages leave and the server keeps having no runtime dependency; in WAL mode with `synchronous=NORMAL` the database "is always consistent" and both rows of a trade live in one file, so a power cut rolls back both or neither ([SQLite](https://www.sqlite.org/pragma.html#pragma_synchronous)). Snapshots stay files: 4.5 MB a minute through a write-ahead log would be gigabytes a day.

## Conclusion

The plan's safety does not come from caution about the new netcode; it comes from an accident of the old one. Lockstep obliges every client to hold the exact truth, and that makes the system being retired the test oracle for its replacement: for as long as both exist, every joined game is a free verifier of every byte the server would send it. That window closes at gate (d), so the order of work is arranged to spend it well, with the codec, the interest sets, the event routing and every summary proven in shadow before lockstep's deletion removes the proof.

The schedule's risk sits in three places, and none of them is the wire format. The read seam through `game.js` decides whether replication takes months or a year. The scoped `Host` decides whether Stage 4 is weeks or a rewrite. The third was the law of typed chat between strangers, which the United Kingdom is still writing, and decision 12 changes its character. With Nanny Mode, gate (b) no longer waits on a regulator's timetable, because a player connecting from a listed country starts on quick phrases whatever the final text says. What the law now decides is narrower, and it costs money rather than time: whether the scope must widen from public worlds to all, and what a sound age check at 16 costs a hobby server, for which no free route was found. The lawyer's review and three providers' quotes are therefore best sought as soon as the UK text is laid. Until `3-36` and the last step of `6D-6`, nothing in the programme is irreversible: every slice has a setting that restores today's game, and the owner can stop at any gate with a better 8-player game than the one that started.
